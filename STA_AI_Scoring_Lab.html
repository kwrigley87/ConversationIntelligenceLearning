03 · Curveballs

Three questions designed to catch you out

These are deliberately believable. The trick is distinguishing something mentioned in a conversation from something actually proven by the conversation.

Curveball 01 · The invisible action

“Did the agent check the billing system before answering?”
Not observable from the interaction alone. The conversation may contain the answer the agent gave, but that does not prove which system they checked.

Curveball 02 · The vague judgement

“Did the agent provide good customer service?”
Needs rework. “Good customer service” needs observable criteria. Break the intent into behaviours or outcomes the interaction can actually evidence.

Curveball 03 · The process claim

“Did the agent follow all company procedures?”
Needs rework. This is too broad. Narrow it to a specific observable requirement that can be evidenced in the conversation.
Golden Nugget: Don't ask AI to score something simply because the answer would be useful. Ask whether the interaction contains enough evidence to support the answer.

05 · Takeaway

Before you enable AI Scoring...

Use this five-question check whenever you look at a traditional form.

1 · Observable?Can the interaction contain the evidence?
2 · Specific?Is there one clear thing to score?
3 · Defensible?Would two reviewers understand the evidence similarly?
4 · Explainable?Could we explain why the answer was selected?
5 · Validated?Have we compared AI results with human review?

The point isn't “AI can score this.”

The point is: “AI can score this reliably enough, from evidence we can observe, and we know how to validate it.”

Your next move

Take one traditional evaluation question from your current customer context and run it through the five checks. If it fails one, rewrite it before thinking about scale.
