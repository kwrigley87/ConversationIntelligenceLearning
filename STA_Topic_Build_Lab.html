01 · Mission

Build, test and improve a Cancellation Intent topic

Your customer wants to reliably identify when a customer is expressing an intention to cancel. Your job is to turn that business intent into a usable topic, test it against real conversations and improve it.

Your mission

Work in Genesys Cloud. You are not trying to create the perfect topic first time. Build something sensible, test the evidence, then tune it.

Business intent
Identify customers expressing an intention to cancel or leave the service.
Success
The topic finds genuine cancellation intent without simply matching every use of words such as “cancel” or “leave”.
02 · Build the topic

Start in Genesys Cloud

Use the Topic Library and make the design decisions yourself. There is deliberately no “perfect phrase list” here.

Create the topic

Name it Cancellation Intent. Use a clear description/objective so another analyst knows exactly what the topic is intended to detect.

Create 5–8 phrases

Think about how real customers express cancellation intent. Include different wording and natural variations — not just keywords.

Choose the participant

Decide whose language should trigger the topic. Ask yourself whether you are trying to detect customer intent, agent behaviour, or both.

Choose an initial strictness

Pick a sensible starting point. You will validate it later; do not treat strictness as a “make it accurate” slider.

Connect it to the right Program

Decide where this topic should be searched for. Confirm the relevant queue/flow and Program relationship.
Before you publish: read every phrase and ask, “If I heard this phrase in isolation, would it reliably support the topic objective?”
03 · Test & tune

Test & tune your topic

Use Topic Phrase Testing / available historical interactions. Your first version is a hypothesis — testing tells you whether it works.

Test these phrases

Use Topic Phrase Testing to review how these phrases behave and compare the confidence returned for each match.

PHRASE 1
“Cancel my policy”
PHRASE 2
“Go ahead and cancel”
PHRASE 3
“Cancel my order”
PHRASE 4
“Cancel the account”
PHRASE 5
“Cancel my cable”
What to look for: Review the confidence and transcript context for each phrase. Consider whether the match supports the Cancellation Intent topic objective and what you would tune based on the results.
Separate confidence test — not part of the topic:

Test the phrase “issue with bill” with strictness set to 1. Use this to observe the phrase confidence returned and how strictness influences matching. Do not add this phrase to the Cancellation Intent topic.

Your tuning task

KEEPIdentify one phrase that is producing useful, relevant matches.
CHANGE / REMOVEFind one phrase that is too broad, ambiguous or creating poor matches.
ADDFind one genuine way customers express the intent that your first version missed.

Then test again. Review the matched transcript context, not just whether the phrase technically appeared.

04 · Extend it

Go beyond a single topic

Once the topic is credible, use the wider Conversation Intelligence tools to make the signal more useful.

Topic Miner

If suitable data is available, explore how customers actually talk about cancellation. Compare discovered language with the phrases you invented.

Create an Interaction Category

Build a more specific signal using your Cancellation Intent topic with additional criteria. For example, explore how you could identify a higher-risk cancellation scenario using other conversational evidence.

Find the evidence

Use Content Search / relevant views to locate interactions containing your topic and inspect the conversations behind the signal.
05 · Takeaway

What good topic design looks like

  • Start with the business intent, not a list of keywords.
  • Use language customers actually use, then validate it against real conversations.
  • Context matters. A word appearing does not mean the intended event occurred.
  • Tune for the use case, not for 100% coverage.
  • Test → review → improve → repeat. Topic design is iterative.
